## Quick Verdict

Useful and directly relevant.

The strongest reusable points are:

- The "scripts/workflows versus skills/agents" objection should be handled by asking where deterministic workflow tools stop and reasoning becomes valuable.
- n8n-style or node-based automation tools are useful for linear workflows; the OpenAI wedge is complex triage, changing SOPs, edge cases, judgment, and reusable skills.
- A demo should not become a feature tour. Start from business motivation, buyer pain, and proof needed; keep product showing short unless the buyer is still engaged.
- Watch attention signals during demos. If the room is bored or multitasking, stop showing and return to discovery.
- Build a memorized objection bank from calls. Rehearse the most common objections until the answer sounds confident and the discovery path naturally leads into the prepared answer.
- Confidence, tone, and agenda-setting matter. Sales calls need a different energy from normal conversation, but the live version should be professional rather than theatrical.

## Reusable Interview Prep

Use this source to sharpen four answer clusters:

1. **Workspace Agents versus n8n/workflow tools**: "I would not attack deterministic workflow tools. I would ask where the workflow is linear versus where it needs judgment, changing SOPs, exception handling, and human review."
2. **Demo strategy**: "Before showing the product, I would ask what the buyer needs to see for the workflow to be credible. Then I would show only that, keep it short, and return to discovery."
3. **Objection handling**: "I would build an objection bank from real calls, group objections by buyer type, then rehearse the top objections until I can answer calmly and use discovery to surface the right one."
4. **Technical-buyer credibility**: "With a technical buyer, the answer is not to pretend to know everything. It is to ask sharper workflow questions, separate deterministic automation from reasoning work, bring technical support when needed, and avoid making exact product claims casually."
