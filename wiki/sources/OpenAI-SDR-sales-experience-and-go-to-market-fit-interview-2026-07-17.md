## What The Interviewer Was Testing

The interview began with a broad career recap, then narrowed sharply onto one concern:

> Is Seth primarily a technically capable builder who would fit go-to-market engineering, or does he have enough repeatable sales experience for this Strategic BDR role?

The interviewer tested this through quota recency, Airwallex hunting and prospecting, company size, Boxo buyer personas and ICP, enterprise account scale, and whether Seth personally sourced and closed a Boxo opportunity.

## Evidence That Landed

### Airwallex

- Seth clearly confirmed he hunted new business and had only worked full-cycle motions.
- He explained why outbound was needed: inbound and channel leads were small, inconsistent, or higher KYC risk.
- He described selecting travel based on evidence from more mature Airwallex markets.
- He mapped finance, operations, and commercial pain separately.
- He described door knocking after LinkedIn and cold email failed, which made the story memorable and showed initiative.
- He explained champion creation, CEO approval, cautious rollout, and expansion.

### Boxo

- Seth explained the technical product and build-versus-buy frame credibly.
- After redirection, he named the buyer personas directly: engineering, product, and partnerships/commercial.
- He described a signal-led ICP: large apps, banks, wallets, super apps, exchanges, download/user scale, and public expansion intent.
- He showed persona-specific messaging rather than one generic outbound note.
- Named account types such as banks, Grab, Shopee, Binance, and major APAC wallets made the enterprise scale more legible.
- He described sourcing an Unabank / U-A-N-A Financial opportunity through a commercial expansion signal and pre-aligning a potential eSIM partner.

### AI curiosity

- Seth's AI interest came across as lived and specific: Salescraft, scraping workflows, Cursor, Clay, Sample Healthcare, Codex, and current workflow projects.
- The interviewer, who identified as a former sales-rep-turned-builder/product operator, engaged deeply with this background.

## Friction And Credibility Risks

### Answering around the question

The interviewer had to redirect Seth several times: from AI/building back to sales; from Airwallex personas to company type; from Boxo product explanation to the literal prospecting-personas question; and from broad enterprise examples to the largest sourced deal and exact company.

The substance was usually present, but it arrived after too much context.

### Metrics and definitions

These may reflect transcription errors, basis mismatches, or live approximation. Do not treat them as verified facts.

### Builder-versus-seller positioning

The opening recap over-weighted AI building, workflow implementation, and technical tools. That prompted the interviewer to say Seth might be an excellent fit for go-to-market engineering, which was not the open role.

Seth recovered by explicitly stating that sales was the governing interest and by proving quota, hunting, prospecting, and full-cycle ownership. Future versions should lead with those proofs so the concern never forms.

### Question quality

The role-scope question was useful. The tooling question was riskier because it referenced a friend's claim that OpenAI had “no tooling,” which created an unnecessary hearsay frame and reopened the builder theme after the interviewer had already resolved it.
