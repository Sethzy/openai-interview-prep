# OpenAI SDR Interview With Sam — Product And Technical Fluency

## Scope

- Interviewer: Sam, described in the transcript as Singapore-based and focused on digital-native businesses.
- Interview type: product and technical fluency.
- Transcript boundary: the Sam interview begins after the recruiter handoff and ends when Andrew joins; the explicit handoff occurs at raw transcript lines 118–120.
- Parent raw transcript: `.raw/interviews/openai-sdr-product-fluency-and-sales-acumen-2026-07-22.txt`
- Source quality: automated transcript. Product names, model versions, company facts, and exact metrics require verification.

## Questions Tested

1. Career introduction.
2. Explain a technical concept simply.
3. How Seth uses AI today.
4. Autonomous agents and automations.
5. AI use in the Strategic BDR role.
6. Three target logos and why.
7. API account-selection signals.
8. Hallucination mitigation.
9. OpenAI models versus Copilot.
10. Candidate questions about product direction and culture.

## Positive Evidence

- Seth's “map of meaning” metaphor made RAG understandable, then he correctly explained embeddings, retrieval, context augmentation, and generation when Sam probed further.
- His AI use was clearly lived rather than rehearsed: second brain, account research, stakeholder segmentation, daily digests, work prioritization, small software tools, and scheduled agents.
- The role-specific answer was commercially useful: AI for account intelligence, ROI calculators, and lightweight demos that help buyers visualize impact.
- Sam explicitly responded to this with “Build and demo. Love that.”
- Seth identified two sensible API-account signals: meaningful internal development capability and ambitions that make scalable or multilingual workflows valuable.
- His hallucination answer covered grounding, internal tools, representative evaluations, sandboxing, and gradual rollout.
- On OpenAI versus Copilot, Seth ultimately reached the distinction Sam wanted: similar underlying models can sit inside different harnesses and product experiences. Sam said, “Correct answer. That's all I want.”
- Seth's product-direction question produced an engaged, detailed response and demonstrated genuine interest in both developer and knowledge-work surfaces.

## Friction

- Sam redirected the AI-use answer from backend mechanics to actual use cases.
- Seth could not deliver three named target accounts cleanly. He offered thoughtful regional and product logic, but only DBS became a clear enterprise logo with a concrete wedge.
- Several market-size, developer-count, contract-value, and competitor statements were guesses or hearsay.
- The initial Copilot response admitted no firsthand use and repeated a negative second-hand opinion before recovering technically.
- Some explanations lacked a final sentence connecting the technical mechanism to the customer's risk, outcome, or decision.

## Direct Outcome Signals

- “Build and demo. Love that.”
- “Great answer” during the target-account discussion, despite later pressure for greater specificity.
- “Correct answer. That's all I want” on the model-versus-harness distinction.
- No explicit advancement or pass statement.
