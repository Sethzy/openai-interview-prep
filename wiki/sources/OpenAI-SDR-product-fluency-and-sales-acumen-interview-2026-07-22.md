This was a **lean pass / likely pass**, with moderate rather than high confidence because neither interviewer confirmed advancement.

## Panel 1 — Product And Technical Fluency

### What landed

- The “map of meaning” explanation of RAG was understandable and technically grounded.
- Seth showed lived AI use: the wiki/second brain, account research, stakeholder segmentation, small software tools, scheduled digests, daily prioritization, and proof-of-work demos.
- The role-specific answer connected AI to account intelligence, stakeholder research, ROI calculators, and lightweight demos.
- The DBS support wedge was concrete: improve rule-based chat and IVR experiences with conversational and voice systems.
- The hallucination answer named RAG, internal tools, representative eval sets, sandboxing, and staged rollout.
- The final OpenAI-versus-Copilot distinction correctly separated the underlying model from the product harness and experience.

### What hurt

- When asked how Seth uses AI, the answer initially became a backend architecture explanation. Sam had to say: focus on the use cases.
- The three-logo question was the clearest miss. Seth gave market-selection logic but could not provide three researched, named accounts with a crisp reason for each.
- Several market and contract-value statements were presented as guesses or hearsay. They weakened otherwise thoughtful account logic.
- “I have never actually used Copilot” followed by a negative second-hand opinion was avoidable. The later harness answer recovered the substance.
- The hallucination answer needed a cleaner business-value close: define the risky task, constrain the agent, require evidence or abstention, add human review for consequential actions, monitor production behavior, and expand only after passing agreed thresholds.

## Panel 2 — Sales Acumen

### What landed

- The opening career narrative was sales-first: Boxo enterprise sourcing and closing, Airwallex quota attainment, then AI-native work and the OpenAI motivation.
- The Fortune 500 plan was strong on workflow hypotheses, evidence gathering, stakeholder mapping, Account Director alignment, tailored outreach, adjacent proof points, and a lightweight demo.
- Andrew explicitly praised using OpenAI tools both to brainstorm the account and to build a POC/MVP for outreach.
- The learning answer showed genuine product curiosity through daily experimentation rather than abstract interest.
- The UNA Financial story was the strongest answer of the hour: clear sourcing signal, champion, build-versus-buy resistance, failure to multi-thread early, executive intervention, direct technical alignment, and commercial result.
- Seth's questions about OpenAI's enterprise direction and culture produced detailed, engaged answers from Andrew.

### What hurt

- The 30-day account answer was strategically good but did not actually sequence the first 30 days or define outputs and success measures by week.
- The Copilot objection began with product features, benchmark/performance claims, commercial speculation, and price discovery. Andrew had to coach the answer back to priorities, gaps, friction, and customer self-evaluation.
- Calling the personal skill gap “being non-technical” undersold Seth immediately after he rated himself near 8/10 in AI fluency. The intended gap is technical depth and precision, not being non-technical.
- The UNA Financial metric was stated as USD 250K fixed and about USD 350K by departure. The current verified boundary is approximately USD 200K ARR initially and approximately USD 300K after expansion. Reconcile before reusing.
- Saying the Philippines trip was framed as “casually dropping by” can sound manipulative. The cleaner truth is that Seth created an in-person executive and technical alignment meeting to unblock the deal.
