## 2. Five Ways I Use AI

- **Account research:** I use AI to turn public signals, CRM context and stakeholder information into an account hypothesis and buyer map.
- **Customer demos:** I use AI to build lightweight, account-specific demos and prototypes that make the opportunity tangible.
- **Visual communication:** I use AI to turn complex technical workflows into simple diagrams and slide decks that executives can understand quickly.
- **Automated organization:** I use scheduled automations to review my email and calendar, triage priorities and surface what needs my attention.
- **Thought partnership:** I use AI as a sparring partner to challenge my assumptions, rehearse decisions and sharpen my thinking before I act.

## 4. Account Tiering

### Territory boundary and ICP signals

| Item               | Working definition                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Proactive emphasis | Strongly encouraged to concentrate manual outbound on Tier 1 and Tier 2 while retaining scaled coverage for Tier 3                                                                  |
| Travel indicators  | Advertised destinations and large marketing spend on travel fairs.                                                                                                                  |
| Retail indicators  | More than ten stores in strong central locations was a useful scale signal; manufacturing origin on the e-commerce site indicated supplier corridors and cross-border payment needs |

### Airwallex tiering account score

| Criterion                   |   Points | Pre-meeting evidence                                                                                                       |
| --------------------------- | -------: | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Commercial scale            |      0–3 | Company footprint, store count, tour breadth or other observable indicators of meaningful payment volume                   |
| International payment needs |      0–2 | Overseas suppliers, multiple currencies, difficult corridors, recurring international payments or time-sensitive execution |
| Product breadth             |      0–2 | One narrow product need versus a credible path across several Airwallex products                                           |
| Reason to act now           |      0–2 | Generic fit, an account-specific hypothesis, or a visible initiative, deadline, expansion or operational trigger           |
| Access and proof            |      0–1 | Identifiable buyer, event interaction, warm path, partner evidence or relevant customer proof                              |
| **Total**                   | **0–10** | **Determines immediate account priority before discovery**                                                                 |

### Score to motion

| Score | Working tier | Primary motion | Manual effort |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 8–10 | Tier 1 | Bespoke one-to-one account plan | Highest |
| 5–7 | Tier 2 | Targeted one-to-few problem campaign | Moderate |
| 0–4 | Tier 3 / nurture | Scaled, opportunistic and signal-triggered coverage | Low until a new signal appears |

### Short answer

> I tier before the meeting using evidence I can actually observe: commercial scale, international payment needs, product breadth, reason to act now, and access or proof. I score that out of ten. An eight-to-ten account gets a bespoke Tier 1 plan; a five-to-seven account gets a targeted Tier 2 campaign; and lower-scoring accounts remain in scaled or signal-based coverage. Discovery then replaces those proxies with validated economics and buyer evidence.

## 5. Tier-Specific Execution

| Dimension | Tier 1: one-to-one | Tier 2: one-to-few | Tier 3: scaled |
|---|---|---|---|
| Planning unit | One named strategic account | Small cohort sharing the same problem | Broad segment or nurture pool |
| Research | Account, initiatives, workflow, buying group and relationship paths | Common vertical problem plus one company-specific signal | Basic fit and automated or event-derived signals |
| Message | Bespoke point of view for the account and stakeholder | Reusable problem narrative with a personalized opening | Broad value proposition or trigger-based message |
| Stakeholders | Map the full committee; sequence two to four relevant entry points | Start with the most likely owner persona | Engage responders and promote when evidence improves |
| Proof | Closest workflow, region, role and risk match | Reusable vertical/customer proof | Broad proof assets |
| Collateral | Tailored deck, ROI hypothesis or account brief when justified | Campaign asset with light personalization | Standard content |
| Channels | Coordinated multichannel plan; warm paths and direct approaches where appropriate | Repeatable LinkedIn, email, phone and event sequence | Campaigns, events, inbound, referrals and partner channels |
| Goal | Create and advance a strategic opportunity | Efficiently find accounts where the shared hypothesis is real | Capture demand without consuming bespoke capacity |

### Tier 1 example: EU Holidays

| Preparation dimension | What I did |
|---|---|
| Vertical research | Watched UK-team Chorus calls to understand the travel use case |
| Account research | Collected brochures and studied the advertised European and China tours |
| Customer interaction | Spoke with one or two representatives at the travel fair |
| Commercial hypothesis | Connected destination mix to FX, payment reliability, reconciliation and commercial reach |
| Buyer map | Mapped Finance, Operations, Commercial, CFO and CEO |
| Entry strategy | Selected and explicitly requested the Head of Finance |
| Opening | Prepared around EU Holidays’ actual tour destinations and likely payment corridors |
| Proof | Prepared relevant partner evidence and experience with a smaller competitor |
| Collateral | Created a presentation specifically for EU Holidays |
| Value case | Built a preliminary ROI-calculator spreadsheet to validate during discovery |
| Channel strategy | Shifted from unsuccessful email and LinkedIn outreach to a planned direct office approach |

### Tier 2 example

> For a Tier 2 travel account, I would run a repeatable multichannel campaign around overseas supplier payments, FX and reconciliation, then personalize the opening using that company’s destinations. The campaign is reusable; the reason for contacting that account is still specific. EU Holidays went further: it received a full buyer map, bespoke hypothesis, tailored presentation, ROI calculator and planned office approach.

### Tier 3 answer

> I would not normally allocate bespoke manual prospecting to Tier 3 accounts. I would cover them through scaled campaigns, events, inbound interest, referrals, partner channels and signal-based nurture. If a compelling signal appeared, I would reassess and promote the account into deeper coverage.

## 6. Stakeholder Targeting

### Selection rule

> Start with the person most likely to own the active problem or initiative—not automatically the most senior person. Map the whole buying group, initially approach the two to four most relevant stakeholders, and expand access as each decision risk becomes clearer.

| Persona | Likely concern | Message angle |
|---|---|---|
| Finance | Economics, reliability, reconciliation and control | Quantify failure, manual effort, error and financial impact |
| Operations | Continuity and execution risk | Show how the workflow becomes more dependable and less manual |
| Commercial | Growth and market reach | Connect the solution to destinations, customers or revenue opportunities |
| Partnerships | Partner supply, merchant categories and launch velocity | Show how the platform expands partner capability faster |
| Product | User experience, roadmap and launch speed | Connect the wedge to a concrete product outcome |
| Engineering | Feasibility, maintenance and build-versus-buy | Respect technical depth and compare total effort, ownership and speed |
| Security / Legal / Procurement | Data, risk, control and process | Identify the precise question and involve the correct specialist |
| Executive | Strategic outcome, economics, risk and why now | Summarize the business case and decision required |

## 9. Discovery Sequence

|                   Step | What I need to learn                                  | Questions to ask                                                                                                                                           | Output                                         |
| ---------------------: | ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
|            1. Priority | Why this matters now                                  | “What’s happening in the business that has made this a priority now?”                                                                                      | Reason now / compelling event                  |
|            2. Workflow | How the work happens today                            | “How does the work happen today, across which people and systems?” “Where does it slow down, fail or require manual intervention?”                         | Current-state workflow                         |
|       3. Gap and cause | Why the issue persists                                | “What have you already tried, and what prevented that from solving it?”                                                                                    | Root cause and failed alternatives             |
|         4. Consequence | What becomes expensive, risky, delayed or constrained | “Which metric is this affecting today, and where does it need to get to?” “What happens if nothing changes?”                                               | Quantified business consequence                |
|  5. People and process | Who owns and how a decision gets made                 | “Who feels the problem most day to day, and who is accountable for improving it?” “Walk me through how your organization would evaluate and approve this.” | Buying group and decision path                 |
| 6. Proof and next step | What must be true to continue                         | “What evidence would make the change feel credible and safe?”                                                                                              | Proof criteria and buyer-confirmed next action |

## 10. Qualification, Next Steps And Champions

### Five-part qualification

| Element | Must be known | Evidence standard |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | Named workflow, root cause and consequence | Buyer describes the problem in operational terms |
| Priority | Reason now, desired outcome and cost of delay | Active initiative, deadline, risk or quantified consequence |
| People | Owner, champion, technical/risk stakeholders and economic buyer | Named people and their roles in the decision |
| Process | Evaluation, approval, procurement and decision path | Buyer explains how the organization will decide |
| Proof | What the buyer must believe and what remains missing | Smallest credible validation step and success measure |

### SQL/SQO handoff test

| Question | Ready when… |
|---|---|
| Is there a real problem? | The buyer confirms the workflow and consequence |
| Is it a priority? | There is a reason to act and cost to delay |
| Is there an owner? | A credible stakeholder will mobilize the evaluation |
| Is there a path? | The next decision, participants and buying route are visible |
| Can we prove it? | Required evidence and smallest useful proof step are defined |

### Next-step contract

| Element | Confirm before ending the call |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Which decision or missing proof will the step resolve? |
| People | Who must attend because they own that decision? |
| Preparation | What will the buyer and seller each provide beforehand? |
| Owner and date | Who owns each action, and when will the next step occur? |

### Five behavioral champion tests

| Test | Champion evidence |
|---|---|
| Correct the thesis | Shares consequences, incentives and what will not land internally |
| Reveal the process | Names the economic buyer, evaluators, blockers and approval path |
| Co-author the case | Edits proof criteria, value model or internal narrative |
| Create purposeful access | Introduces the stakeholder who owns the next decision risk |
| Mobilize internally | Uses the case without the seller and returns with real objections or commitments |

## 11. Account Advancement

### Weekly goal rule

> “Book a meeting” is not the only goal. Keep a strategic account in focus across multiple weeks and move it through evidence milestones.

| Week | Example strategic objective |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Identify the current workflow and likely initiative owner |
| 2 | Uncover team KPIs and the operational consequence through manager-level conversations |
| 3 | Secure the relevant director or executive conversation using the intelligence gathered |
| 4 | Confirm the buying path and smallest proof required to continue |

## 13. Stalled And Lost Opportunities


### Diagnostic table

| Situation | Likely problem | SDR action |
|---|---|---|
| Contact engaged but nothing progresses | No clear priority or next decision | Recap the problem and propose one dated next step with a purpose |
| Champion goes quiet | Weak champion, internal concern or changed priority | Review the stakeholder map, identify the missing person and coordinate a new access route with the AD |
| Buyer requests generic collateral | They cannot carry the internal case | Ask what internal conversation the material must support; provide a concise, forwardable account-specific case |
| Technical or governance concern appears | Missing proof or specialist credibility | Capture the exact question and owner; bring in the AD or technical specialist |
| Correct account, wrong contact | Stakeholder-selection failure | Change persona using a message tied to that stakeholder’s responsibility |
| Strong fit, wrong timing | No present compelling event | Nurture with a reason, trigger and review date |
| No meaningful pain or consequence | Curiosity rather than an opportunity | Disqualify cleanly and replenish the territory |
| Buyer chooses incumbent or internal build | Decision criteria favored another path | Record the decision, economic buyer, winning criteria and possible reopening trigger |
| Opportunity is formally lost | No viable current path | Close the loop, capture the learning and update the account or campaign playbook |

### Lost-deal answer

> At Boxo, Binance ultimately chose to build internally. My lesson was that technical interest alone was insufficient: I needed to qualify build-versus-buy earlier and reach the economic buyer with a stronger case around total ownership, maintenance and speed. In an SDR role, I would capture the winning criteria and reopening trigger, feed that learning into the campaign, and coordinate any future re-entry with the Account Director.
