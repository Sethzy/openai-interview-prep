# OpenAI SDR Sales Acumen Interview Debrief — 2026-07-22

## Rating

**8/10 — likely pass and the stronger of the two interviews.**

Seth fixed the main July 17 objection, delivered a differentiated account strategy, and told a strong full-cycle deal story. Andrew's praise was specific rather than generic. The Copilot objection was a real miss, but it was one coached answer inside an otherwise convincing sales interview.

## What Went Well

### The sales identity was clear immediately

Seth led with Boxo enterprise prospecting and closing, Airwallex quota performance, then AI-native work. Unlike July 17, the interviewer never wondered whether Seth belonged in GTM engineering rather than sales.

This is the most important improvement across the interview process.

**Rating: 8/10.**

### The Fortune 500 account approach was strategically strong

Seth moved through workflow hypotheses, evidence gathering, problem prioritization, stakeholder mapping, Account Director coordination, tailored messages, adjacent customer proof, and a lightweight demo. Andrew explicitly praised both AI-assisted brainstorming and the POC/MVP prospecting approach.

The answer demonstrated how Seth could improve an Account Director's account plan rather than merely book meetings.

**Rating: 8.5/10.**

### The UNA Financial answer was excellent

This was the best answer across both interviews. Seth covered:

- The public expansion signal that made the account worth pursuing.
- Why the head of partnerships became the initial champion.
- Proof of work through pre-researched merchant and eSIM relationships.
- The build-versus-buy resistance from product and engineering.
- His mistake: relying too heavily on a commercial champion and multi-threading too late.
- His recovery: creating direct executive and technical alignment in person.
- The move from internal misalignment to a proper technical evaluation.

Andrew called the answer “really good.” It proved sourcing, discovery, stakeholder judgment, self-awareness, initiative, and closing ownership in one story.

**Rating: 9/10.**

### The development answer showed coachability and initiative

Seth identified a real development goal—greater technical depth—and backed it with daily experimentation, product evaluation, small builds, and sales-automation workflows. That sounds more credible than naming a generic weakness.

**Rating: 8/10.**

### Product enthusiasm remained human

The story about helping his mother research air conditioners made the value of an accessible cloud agent easy to understand. The voice-mode example showed how Seth uses the product to think and prepare, not merely because he likes the brand.

**Rating: 8.5/10.**

### The closing questions were strong

The enterprise-direction question connected developer adoption, knowledge work, and custom deployment. Andrew gave a substantial strategic answer. The culture question then produced an unusually candid description of speed, ambiguity, and workload.

**Rating: 8/10.**
