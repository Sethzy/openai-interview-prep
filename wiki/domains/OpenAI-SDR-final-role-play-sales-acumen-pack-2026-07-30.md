# OpenAI SDR Final Role-Play Sales Acumen Pack

## Purpose

This is the focused preparation pack for the final-final interview. The working assumption is that the interviewer will test whether Seth can already think and operate like an OpenAI Strategic BDR, rather than asking only for retrospective career stories.

This pack is **sales acumen only**. It has two jobs:

1. Prepare Seth to role-play territory design, outbound prospecting, campaign strategy, stakeholder entry, and regional judgment at OpenAI.
2. Make Seth fluent enough in fintech infrastructure to explain his Airwallex experience credibly to an interviewer with Stripe experience, culminating in a canonical TruTrip competitive-win story.

It does not replace [[OpenAI-SDR-final-sales-acumen-interview-pack]]. That document remains the evidence bank. This document is the narrow final-round rehearsal surface.

## How To Answer In The Interview

Use the same four-part structure for most questions:

1. **Answer the literal question immediately.**
2. **Give the framework or commercial judgment.**
3. **Prove it with one Airwallex, Boxo, or Salescraft example.**
4. **Translate it into what Seth would do at OpenAI.**

Target 45–90 seconds unless the interviewer asks Seth to role-play the full workflow.

## Part I — OpenAI Role-Play: Core Questions

These are the six highest-priority questions Seth explicitly expects.

### 1. Which markets have you sold into, and what did each market teach you?

**Answer must cover:**

- Airwallex: Singapore territory and full-cycle ownership.
- Boxo: broader APAC strategic-account exposure.
- Concrete market examples without pretending every country was owned identically.
- The lesson that APAC is a set of distinct markets, not one uniform region.
- How that experience transfers into OpenAI's APAC territory.

### 2. What is your outbound prospecting approach?

**Answer must cover:**

- Territory → ICP/anti-ICP → account tiering → trigger or hypothesis → stakeholder map → first touch → multi-channel cadence → discovery → qualification → Account Director handoff.
- Separate one-to-one strategic pursuits from one-to-few campaigns and scaled coverage.
- Use AI for research, prioritization, and preparation rather than indiscriminate volume.
- One proof point from Airwallex or Boxo.

### 3. Is top-down or bottom-up prospecting better?

**Working position:** Neither is universally better. The strongest enterprise motion connects both.

**Answer must distinguish:**

- **Top-down:** creates executive sponsorship, budget, urgency, governance, and permission to change.
- **Bottom-up:** exposes real workflow pain, produces champions, validates adoption, and creates product proof.
- Start from whichever side has the strongest signal, then multi-thread quickly.
- For OpenAI, usage or practitioner pull can create the bottom-up evidence; executive alignment converts it into a governed enterprise deployment.

### 4. What outbound campaign would you run at OpenAI?

**Answer must cover:**

- One market, one vertical, one workflow, one buying group, and one proof point.
- Why now and what observable signals identify the target accounts.
- Target-account rules and anti-ICP.
- Stakeholder-specific messages.
- A bounded discovery or proof step.
- Campaign success metrics and the learning loop.

**Recommended rehearsal campaign:** Singapore financial-services customer operations or developer productivity. Choose one before the interview; do not present two unfocused campaigns.

### 5. What regional nuances would change your approach?

**Answer must cover:**

- APAC cannot be treated as one market.
- Singapore: regional HQs, regulated industries, clearer enterprise buyer maps, and regional reference value.
- Indonesia: scale and conglomerate/platform opportunity, with greater need for local relationships and economic-context awareness.
- Vietnam: technical talent and product/API opportunity, with language, relationship, and local-partner considerations.
- Philippines: customer operations, voice, BPO/IT-BPM, and English-language operating strength.
- Thailand: practical fintech adoption and strong local-market context.
- Nuances are hypotheses to verify at the account level, not stereotypes.

### 6. What would your first 30, 60 and 90 days look like if you joined OpenAI?

> I would structure the first 90 days around calibration, execution and repeatability. I would make the plan concrete, but I would calibrate any meeting or pipeline target with my manager rather than pretend I know OpenAI's ramp quota from the outside.
>
> In the first 30 days, I would learn the actual motion. I would get fluent in the product, security and trust fundamentals; shadow Account Directors across different account situations; and align with the manager, Account Directors, solutions or deployment partners, and the people who own product signals and pipeline operations. I would review current account plans, strong and weak opportunities, existing relationships and product usage before deciding where to focus. By day 30, I would want an agreed territory thesis, a prioritized account list, three deep account plans, an initial stakeholder map and one campaign ready or already beginning to run.
>
> From days 31 to 60, I would turn that preparation into live execution. With the relevant Account Directors, I would run targeted multi-channel outreach, test workflow-specific points of view, lead discovery at the level expected for the role and document the business priority, owner, buying group, enterprise-readiness questions and next step. By day 60, I would expect live conversations in priority accounts, early qualified opportunities that the Account Directors consider worth advancing, and enough conversion data to know which messages, personas and signals are working.
>
> From days 61 to 90, I would make the useful parts repeatable. I would deepen multi-threading in the strongest accounts, inspect where the funnel is breaking, codify one or two vertical or signal-led plays and contribute one narrow, approved AI-enabled GTM workflow—such as account research, meeting preparation, follow-up memory or signal monitoring—with human review. By day 90, success would mean I am contributing qualified pipeline, Account Directors trust my account judgment and handoffs, and the team has one documented improvement it can actually reuse.

**Concrete ramp outputs:**

| Period | Primary objective | Tangible outputs | Evidence of progress |
|---|---|---|---|
| Days 1–30 | Calibrate | Product and trust fluency; internal partner map; territory thesis; prioritized account list; three deep account plans; first campaign and stakeholder maps | Manager and ADs agree that the account logic, messages and handoff standard are credible |
| Days 31–60 | Execute with feedback | Targeted outreach live; relevant meetings and discovery; buyer maps; documented priorities, gaps, competitors and next steps; first qualified opportunities | ADs choose to advance the opportunities and conversion data shows where the motion is working or failing |
| Days 61–90 | Build repeatability | Deeper Tier 1 penetration; repeatable vertical or signal plays; qualified pipeline contribution; one governed AI-enabled GTM workflow | Trusted handoffs, stronger conversion, useful pipeline and a documented improvement the team reuses |

**If pressed for exact numbers:**

> I would ask for the manager's ramp quota and work backward from it. Before I know that, I would not invent ten meetings or five opportunities as if they were OpenAI's standard. In month one I would measure capability and account coverage; in month two, relevant conversations, discovery conversion and opportunity quality; and by month three, performance against the team's actual ramp target plus Account Director trust.

> [!warning] Truth boundary
> This is Seth's proposed operating plan. It is not a claim about OpenAI's onboarding curriculum, activity quota, meeting target, pipeline target or internal account book.

## Part III — Fintech Fluency

The goal is to explain the commercial and operational system, not recite fintech vocabulary.

### A. Payment Gateway

#### 41. What is a payment gateway?

**Answer target:** The technology layer that securely carries a customer's payment details from checkout into the payment-processing chain and returns an approval or decline to the merchant.

#### 42. Walk me through a card payment behind the scenes.

**Required sequence:**

Customer checkout → gateway/tokenization → merchant acquirer or processor → card network → issuing bank authorization → approval/decline → capture → clearing and settlement → merchant receives funds.

#### 43. What is the difference between authorization, capture, and settlement?

#### 44. What do 3DS and a risk engine do?

#### 45. What is a chargeback, and why does it matter commercially?

#### 46. Why might a payment provider hold a reserve or delay settlement?

#### 47. Why do local payment methods matter in APAC?

#### 48. How do payment acceptance, FX, and settlement interact when the customer and merchant use different currencies?

### B. FX And Transfers

#### 49. What happens behind the scenes in a cross-border transfer?

**Required sequence:**

Funding account → FX quote and conversion when required → payment instruction → local clearing rail or SWIFT/correspondent-bank path → beneficiary bank → reconciliation and final status.

#### 50. What is an FX spread?

#### 51. Why can a bank's headline transfer fee understate the real cost?

#### 52. What is the difference between SWIFT and a local payment rail?

#### 53. Why can the beneficiary receive less money than the sender intended?

#### 54. Why are speed, transparency, and reconciliation as important as the FX rate?

#### 55. How does a multi-currency wallet reduce duplicate conversions?

#### 56. When should a business use a transfer rather than a card?

### C. Cards

#### 57. How does a corporate or virtual card work behind the scenes?

**Required actors:**

Cardholder or employee → merchant/acquirer → card network → issuer/issuer processor → authorization controls → clearing and settlement → transaction feed and reconciliation.

#### 58. What is the difference between an issuer, acquirer, card network, and payment gateway?

#### 59. Why are virtual cards useful for travel companies?

#### 60. How do spend limits, merchant controls, approval policies, and single-use cards reduce risk?

#### 61. How does a card issuer make money?

#### 62. Why might a company choose cards instead of bank transfers for some supplier payments?

#### 63. How do card transactions connect to expense management and accounting reconciliation?

### D. Airwallex Versus Stripe

#### 64. How did you position Airwallex against Stripe during your tenure?

**Historical answer frame:**

- Do not argue that one platform is universally better.
- Identify the customer's exact workflow.
- Stripe could be strong for payment acceptance and developer experience.
- Airwallex's historical wedge was often the combined cross-border operating system: multi-currency collection, holding balances, FX, transfers, cards, and reconciliation.
- The value case depended on preventing unnecessary conversions, improving corridor coverage, and connecting collections to downstream supplier payments or card spend.

#### 65. When would Stripe still have been the better fit?

#### 66. How do you sell against a respected incumbent without attacking it?

#### 67. What evidence must you collect before claiming that switching providers will save money?
