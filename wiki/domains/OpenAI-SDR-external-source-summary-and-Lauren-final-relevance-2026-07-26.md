### 01 — Harrison Chase: Own your intelligence

- **Source:** [source](https://x.com/hwchase17/status/2081002647814094888)

- **Summary:** The post argues that companies putting AI into critical products and workflows should retain control of their operational intelligence and improvement loops.
- **Relevance / takeaway:** **High** — Useful enterprise framing: buyers care about control, learning, governance, and durable workflow advantage, not model access alone.

### 02 — Sam Altman: How to Start a Startup

- **Source:** [talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vv3CEAS_w34); [originating post](https://x.com/ti_morse/status/2081068675834994774)

- **Summary:** Altman argues that AI has reset startup speed and the competitive baseline; founders should build for continuing model improvement, learn to operate through chaos, use mission to choose among good projects, and judge trends by deep repeated use. He uses OpenAI's early ambiguity and the Codex turnaround as examples of conviction, concentrated execution, and redirecting resources toward the highest-leverage strategic opportunity.
- **Relevance / takeaway:** **High** — Strong Lauren-final framing for ambiguity, pace, prioritization, and high-level AI acumen. Use the Codex story to explain why OpenAI values people who can execute without a finished playbook, while attributing product-performance and internal-resource claims to Altman; Seth's own Airwallex, Boxo, Salescraft, and Second Brain examples remain the proof.

### 07 — InvestInAssets: Seven sales principles

- **Source:** [X post](https://x.com/InvestInAssets/status/2080986202098811101); [original HN comment](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=23285438)

- **Summary:** The seven-point note argues for action over overcomplication, people-and-problem-first selling, urgent pain over optional benefit, trust built through authentic usefulness, and complete separation between the seller's internal targets and the buyer's problem.
