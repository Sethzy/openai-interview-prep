## What Hurt

### 1. The literal answer arrived late

The recurring pattern was:

`question → background/context → adjacent insight → interviewer redirect → literal answer`

The next-round pattern should be:

`literal answer → one proof point → optional expansion`

Examples:

- “Who were the personas?” → “Head of engineering, head of product, and head of partnerships. Engineering cared about build-versus-buy...”
- “What companies?” → “Singapore mid-market travel, retail, and ecommerce companies. The verified threshold was roughly X; I would not overstate employee count.”
- “When did you last carry quota?” → “About 18 months ago at Airwallex. Since then I have still sold full-cycle as a founder, but Airwallex was my last formal quota.”

### 2. The career spine was upside down

For this role, the order should be:

1. Boxo: enterprise platform sales and full-cycle ownership.
2. Airwallex: formal quota and repeatable outbound execution.
3. Founder/AI work: continued full-cycle selling plus technical fluency.
4. OpenAI fit: return to strategic enterprise sales.

### 3. Unverified precision sounded less credible than a caveat

The interviewer challenged the “number one travel agency” analogy, mid-market definition, company size, and Unabank name. When details are uncertain, a clean caveat is stronger:

> “I want to separate the verified result from the remembered basis. I closed 22 logos and finished above 200% annual quota. Average deal size was roughly in the USD 40K–60K range, but I would verify the exact basis rather than overstate it.”

### 4. Some language undersold seniority

Phrases such as “silly enough,” “got lucky,” “little hack,” “babysit,” “jank,” and “technically missed it” added personality but accumulated into a less controlled executive impression.

Keep warmth, but replace them with:

- “I took a founder grant and started testing the thesis.”
- “The door-knocking experiment gave me two live conversations and validated the pain.”
- “I personally managed the initial rollout and risk.”
- “Boxo did not use a formal individual quota then; the commercial result was...”

### 5. The final pitch missed a listening cue

The interviewer had already given positive feedback and invited questions. Seth tried to sell again. This was not damaging, but the better move was to accept the signal and ask one high-quality question.

## Rules For The Next Interview

1. Answer the noun first: persona, company, quota date, deal size, or personal role.
2. Keep the first answer to 20–40 seconds; let the interviewer pull more detail.
3. Lead with sales evidence before AI-building evidence.
4. Use one metric per sentence and state its basis.
5. Separate verified facts from remembered estimates aloud.
6. Do not use analogies where a category or threshold would be clearer.
7. Stop selling once the interviewer says they are convinced.
8. Ask questions about success, account ownership, AD partnership, and evaluation—not internal-tool gossip.

## Best Next-Round Questions

- “What distinguishes the Strategic BDRs whom Account Directors trust most in their first six months?”
- “Where do you expect the BDR to keep ownership after an opportunity is accepted—stakeholder mapping, discovery follow-up, account planning, or later deal calls?”
- “How do you evaluate readiness for broader account ownership, given the role is designed as a launchpad but progression depends on performance and business need?”
