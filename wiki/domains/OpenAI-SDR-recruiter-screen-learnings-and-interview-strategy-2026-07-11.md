# OpenAI SDR Interview — Recruiter Recommendations By Phase

## How To Read This

This page follows the interview process in the order the recruiter explained it. Under each phase:

- **What happens** is the format described by the recruiter.
- **What the recruiter recommended** stays close to her wording and intent.
- **What this means for Seth** is our preparation translation, clearly separated from her direct guidance.

The transcript is automated and contains errors. The short quotations below have been lightly cleaned for obvious filler and transcription mistakes without changing their meaning.

## Before The Interview Phases — What This Role Actually Is

### What the recruiter said

OpenAI has not previously had an inside-sales team. This is the first team, and the company is targeting “a bit more of a senior profile than what the title implies.” Candidates generally have four to six years of professional experience and at least roughly two years of full-cycle closing experience.

Her reasons were:

- “This is going to be a founding team.”
- They need people comfortable “operating in a more ambiguous environment.”
- People should be ready to “hit the ground running” without much instruction or oversight.
- The team will help “define processes” and shape what inside sales looks like at OpenAI.
- Team members will pair with OpenAI's most strategic Account Directors on “very large, multimillion, multi-thread deals.”
- The main priorities remain “booking meetings and generating pipeline,” but the role should also run meetings, visit customers on-site as needed, and have some ownership inside accounts.
- OpenAI wants an internal talent pipeline, with the intention of promoting people into Account Director roles in “12 to 18 months.”

### What this means for Seth

Frame this as a strategic inside-sales and AD-apprenticeship role, not a normal junior SDR job. Throughout the process, show:

- Full-cycle selling experience.
- Comfort without an established playbook.
- Process-building and initiative.
- Readiness to run customer meetings.
- Enterprise multi-threading and stakeholder ownership.
- Hunger to generate pipeline, not only interest in strategy or product.

## Phase 1 — Seven-Minute Take-Home Video

### What happens

The recruiter described:

- Approximately one to two minutes on “why you” and “why OpenAI.”
- Approximately five minutes on an AI use case for DoorDash.
- The written email contains the detailed questions and requirements.

The actual assignment email remains the authority on exact prompt coverage.

### What the recruiter recommended

#### Follow the prompt carefully

Her recommendation was to read everything carefully and follow the prompt “as stated.”

#### Do not present a generic ChatGPT answer

She warned that many candidates “just copy and paste this into ChatGPT and then present on that.” Her direct advice: “Don't do that.” She said it is obvious when candidates do not bring their own ideas or perspective, and that this is a common reason people fail.

#### Show executive presence

She said the assessment evaluates executive presence:

- How would the candidate perform “in a room full of C-suite executives”?
- How would the candidate perform while presenting to a customer?
- Is the candidate a compelling communicator?
- Does the candidate have good executive presence?

Her delivery advice was not to read from a script and to sound natural.

#### Demonstrate the two traits OpenAI values most

She named two major traits:

1. “Top performers”—people who are hungry, motivated, and like sales.
2. People with “really strong AI curiosity.”

She mentioned that some candidates had built things with Codex and sent them to the team. She explicitly said Seth did not have to do that, but suggested that using AI for visuals could be a good idea.

#### Treat DoorDash like a real existing customer

After Seth noted that DoorDash was already an OpenAI customer, she confirmed that he could build an expansion or more ambitious project around that reality.

Her recommended frame was close to:

> If you were hired, DoorDash was your customer, and you knew they already had OpenAI, how would you target different stakeholders, create an executive engagement plan, and develop a use case?

She said it was acceptable to invent some things, but the work should be based on the fact that DoorDash is already a customer.

### What this means for Seth

- The video must feel like Seth's judgment, not a research memo narrated aloud.
- Use the current DoorDash relationship as the starting point and propose expansion.
- Clearly label assumptions instead of presenting invented whitespace as fact.
- Cover the stakeholder map, executive-engagement plan, and use case—not only the product idea.
- Keep slides and any demo subordinate to Seth's communication.
- Show AI curiosity through something concrete, but do not overload the seven minutes.
- Rehearse enough to be concise, while avoiding memorized or scripted delivery.

### Phase 1 pass question

Would a reviewer trust Seth to walk into an executive customer meeting, present an original account point of view naturally, and turn an existing OpenAI relationship into a larger opportunity?

## Phase 2 — Hiring-Leader Call

### What happens

The next phase is a conversation with one of the hiring leaders. The recruiter said the focus would be Seth's experience.

### What the recruiter recommended

#### Lead with tangible sales evidence

Her direction was to “really talk about those tangibles in sales.”

#### Make Seth's transferable experience obvious

She said Seth has many transferable skills, but anticipated one objection from the hiring team: he may not appear to have as much straightforward, one-to-one AE experience as they sometimes seek.

Her core instruction was to make it very easy for the team to “connect the dots” between:

- Seth's success as an AE at Airwallex.
- His founder experience, where he had to be his own AE.

#### Highlight enterprise selling

Because the role works with large enterprise accounts, she said it was “super important” to highlight Seth's enterprise experience, including Boxo.

#### Prepare for AI-acumen questions

She said the team would ask many questions about Seth's AI knowledge and acumen. Her examples included:

- Explain what an LLM is.
- Explain what RAG is.
- Understand OpenAI's platform and offerings.
- Understand how the offerings compare with competitors.
- Explain why a customer would choose one product or approach over another.

One product-comparison example is unclear in the automated transcript, so prepare product-fit judgment broadly rather than memorizing that wording.

### What this means for Seth

Use one simple career line:

`Boxo enterprise platform sales → Airwallex quota-carrying outbound AE → founder-led full-cycle AI sales and deployment.`

For each chapter, be ready to state:

- What Seth sold.
- The account and buyer type.
- Deal size or ACV using verified numbers.
- How he created or sourced the opportunity.
- Which parts of discovery, solution shaping, negotiation, procurement, and close he owned.
- The stakeholders involved.
- The commercial result.

The goal is to remove the concern that Seth is an interesting founder without enough repeatable sales experience.

### Phase 2 pass question

Can the hiring leader quickly conclude that Seth has already owned real enterprise and full-cycle sales work, even though his career does not follow a conventional uninterrupted AE path?

### Observed July 17 outcome

Seth initially strengthened the builder-versus-seller concern with a builder-heavy career recap, but recovered through Airwallex quota/outbound evidence and Boxo enterprise prospecting/full-cycle evidence. The interviewer then said they were convinced and would probably move Seth forward.

The next-round repair is delivery rather than substance: answer the literal question first, lead with Boxo and Airwallex sales proof, and reconcile uncertain metrics before quoting them.

## Phase 3A — Virtual Panel: Sales Acumen

Execution pack: [[OpenAI-SDR-sales-acumen-interview-pack]]

### What happens

The recruiter described a 30-minute sales-acumen interview with an Account Director on the team.

### What the recruiter said they would ask about

- “How would you go about tiering accounts?”
- How do you target stakeholders?
- What is your strategic outbound plan?
- What level of deal ownership have you had?

### What this means for Seth

Prepare speakable examples rather than abstract frameworks:

- How Seth converts a market into Tier 1, Tier 2, and deprioritized accounts.
- Which first-party, product, company, executive, hiring, regulatory, and workflow signals make an account messageable.
- How he maps business, technical, security, legal, procurement, and executive stakeholders.
- How he chooses a first contact and builds a multi-threaded path.
- How outbound changes by stakeholder instead of repeating one message.
- What Seth personally owned after the meeting was booked.
- How he would coordinate with an OpenAI Account Director on strategy, discovery, handoff, and deal progression.

Use Airwallex, Boxo, and a founder-led AI engagement as primary proof. Do not answer only with what a good seller “would” do; show what Seth has done.

### Phase 3A pass question

Would an Account Director trust Seth to prioritize the right enterprise accounts, open them intelligently, run early conversations, map the buying group, and improve a strategic deal rather than merely schedule a meeting?

## Phase 3B — Virtual Panel: Technical Fluency

Execution pack: [[OpenAI-SDR-technical-acumen-interview-pack]]

### What happens

The recruiter described a separate 30-minute technical-fluency interview.

### What the recruiter recommended

#### Explain technical concepts simply

She said this portion focuses on whether Seth can explain “a super technical concept in really simple terms to someone.”

#### Show product-fit judgment

She returned to the idea of explaining why a customer may fit one OpenAI product or approach rather than another. The exact example is ambiguous in the transcript, but the test is clearly about matching the customer's situation to the right solution.

#### Prepare for existing competitors

She suggested thinking through what happens when a customer is already using a competitor product.

#### Always connect technical knowledge to business value

This was her strongest technical-panel instruction:

> “Always root things back into business value.”

She emphasized that OpenAI is hiring sellers. Technical and AI knowledge matter, but so does the ability to connect them to business outcomes and value.

### What this means for Seth

For each topic—LLMs, RAG, APIs, agents, ChatGPT Enterprise, Codex, connectors/MCP, evaluations, and enterprise controls—practice this sequence:

1. Explain it in plain language.
2. Give one customer-workflow example.
3. Identify who uses it and who buys or governs it.
4. Explain when it is and is not a fit.
5. Name the measurable business outcome.
6. Name the implementation or adoption risk.
7. Recommend the next proof step.

For competitor scenarios, diagnose before selling:

- What is already deployed?
- Which workflow does it serve?
- What is working or failing?
- What switching or coexistence costs exist?
- What proof would justify expanding, coexisting, or changing platforms?

### Phase 3B pass question

Can Seth make a technical buyer respect his fluency while making a business executive understand why the decision matters commercially?

The confirmed July 23 invitation is more specific and slightly broader: expect a values-and-motivation conversation with comparative performance proof and a high-level AI sense-check. Use the current invitation as the controlling source.

### What the recruiter recommended

She described this as the easiest final step. She expected questions about:

- Why Seth is excited about the role.
- Culture fit.
- General motivation and alignment.

### Cultural signals the recruiter volunteered

She described a collaborative environment without always-clearly-defined swim lanes:

- “You see a gap, you step up to fill it.”
- That behavior is rewarded.
- Employees can test new capabilities, submit feedback, and have an impact.

### What this means for Seth

Prepare a grounded motivation answer that connects:

- Genuine Codex/OpenAI product use.
- Enjoyment of enterprise selling and customer work.
- Interest in moving companies from AI experimentation to trusted deployment.
- Comfort building a new process in ambiguity.
- Attraction to the strategic AD apprenticeship and growth path.

Do not make the answer primarily about prestige, frontier-lab access, compensation, or the future AD title. The role itself—pipeline, customers, learning, ownership, and team building—must be attractive.

### Phase 4 pass question

Does the global leader believe Seth genuinely wants this work, will thrive in OpenAI's ambiguous collaborative culture, and can grow into a trusted long-term enterprise seller?

## Across Every Phase — The Recruiter's Repeated Signals

These themes appeared across her description and recommendations:

1. **Sales performance and hunger:** OpenAI wants top performers who like sales.
2. **AI curiosity:** Show real use, building, experimentation, and learning.
3. **Enterprise credibility:** Highlight large accounts and multi-stakeholder selling.
4. **Full-cycle ownership:** Make Airwallex and founder-led closing experience explicit.
5. **Executive presence:** Communicate naturally and compellingly.
6. **Business value:** Technical knowledge must lead to business outcomes.
7. **Ambiguity and initiative:** This is a founding team; see gaps and build.

## Recommended Preparation Order

1. Finalize Phase 1 and rehearse the seven-minute DoorDash video naturally.
2. Build the Phase 2 career spine and verified sales-tangibles sheet.
3. Drill Phase 3A account tiering, stakeholder targeting, outbound, and deal ownership.
4. Drill Phase 3B simple technical explanations, product fit, competitors, and business value.
5. Prepare Phase 4 motivation and founding-team culture-fit answers.
