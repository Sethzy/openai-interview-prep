## Target Output

Build a 7-minute-or-less Loom with a simple deck.

Recommended timing:

- 0:00-1:15: why you, why OpenAI, why this role.
- 1:15-2:00: account context and 3-year AI point of view.
- 2:00-3:20: one high-impact use case and user journey.
- 3:20-4:25: technical / workflow architecture, not too deep.
- 4:25-5:30: 30-day executive engagement plan.
- 5:30-6:15: risks, why OpenAI, and why now.
- 6:15-6:50: standout approach and close.

## What The Prompt Actually Tests

The assignment is designed to test:

- passion for AI and OpenAI's mission;
- executive presence and communication;
- strategic thinking and business judgment;
- consultative instincts and customer empathy;
- creativity and resourcefulness;
- ownership mentality and long-term ambition.

The hidden test is whether the candidate acts like a strategic future seller rather than a generic SDR.

## Part 1: Why You, Why OpenAI

Required coverage:

- why OpenAI;
- why this role;
- what makes Seth a strong fit;
- why Seth would thrive in the environment.

Tone:

- conviction;
- self-awareness;
- humility;
- ambition;
- authenticity.

Seth opening should likely use this shape:

1. "I am an APAC enterprise GTM operator who has worked across fintech, platform partnerships, and AI-native sales systems."
2. "The throughline is turning complex, messy buyer environments into qualified, high-conviction opportunities."
3. "OpenAI is the rare place where a strategic BDR role sits close to the frontier of enterprise AI adoption, not just meeting-setting."
4. "I am willing to step into the role because I want to learn OpenAI's enterprise motion properly while bringing regulated-buyer, stakeholder-mapping, and AI-workflow proof."

Do not over-index on "I want to be an AE." The stronger message is that this is the right learning room and the right product surface.

## Part 2: Account Strategy Anatomy

A strong case has these components:

1. Account context: 3-5 specific facts that explain why the account matters.
2. 3-year point of view: what AI should change in the business.
3. One high-impact use case: one workflow, one user journey, one measurable business outcome.
4. Why OpenAI: product capabilities mapped to the workflow, not generic superiority.
5. Technical plausibility: channels, systems, agents/tools, data, governance, human escalation.
6. Executive engagement plan: who to start with, why, and what business priorities to lead with.
7. Credibility plan: how to avoid looking like a generic vendor.
8. Outbound example: one short message that proves account-specific thinking.
9. Risks and objections: what could fail, including "why not OpenAI."
10. Standout approach: what Seth would do that others would likely not do.

## Winning Pattern From IHH

The IHH response is strongest where it:

- starts with concrete business facts;
- picks a high-value workflow, not a generic assistant;
- shows how the workflow creates revenue/capacity upside;
- tells a vivid patient scenario;
- expands from first workflow to adjacent enterprise workflows;
- includes a simple architecture;
- names implementation, adoption, operational, and ethical risks;
- explains the vendor evaluation lens;
- admits why IHH might not choose OpenAI;
- ends with why the bet still makes sense.

This is the tone to imitate: specific, business-led, technically credible, and candid about risk.

## Recommended Deck Structure

Slide 1: Why Me, Why OpenAI

- one sentence identity;
- one sentence OpenAI motivation;
- one sentence role fit.

Slide 2: Account POV

- why this company;
- what AI should change over the next 3 years;
- why now.

Slide 3: High-Impact Use Case

- current pain;
- proposed workflow;
- target users;
- value drivers.

Slide 4: How It Works

- channels;
- agent/workflow layer;
- customer systems;
- OpenAI surface;
- governance and human escalation.

Slide 5: 30-Day Executive Engagement Plan

- target first champion;
- economic buyer;
- technical/security stakeholders;
- first meeting hypothesis;
- credibility assets.

Slide 6: Outreach Example And Standout Approach

- one short outbound message;
- how Seth would use account intelligence / AI workflow to be sharper than generic sellers;
- close.

If the Loom has to stay under 7 minutes, do not exceed 6 slides.

## Executive Engagement Plan Template

First 30 days:

1. Build the account thesis:
   - earnings calls, annual report, AI announcements, customer complaints, job posts, product roadmap, regulatory constraints, existing AI vendors, and technical hiring.
2. Pick the first wedge:
   - one workflow with executive priority, measurable value, and feasible data/system access.
3. Identify three paths:
   - executive buyer;
   - pain-owning champion;
   - technical/governance validator.
4. Earn credibility:
   - use the account's own language;
   - reference specific initiatives;
   - bring a useful hypothesis, not a generic demo ask;
   - acknowledge risks and existing tools.
5. Multi-thread:
   - start with the champion most likely to feel the pain;
   - build upward to economic buyer;
   - involve technical/security early enough to avoid late-stage blockers.

## Outbound Message Template

Keep the message short and account-specific.

Template:

```text
Subject: [Specific workflow] at [Company]

Hi [Name],

I noticed [specific account signal]. My hypothesis is that [business priority] is being constrained by [workflow bottleneck], especially as [market/operational change].

OpenAI may be able to help by [one practical use case], starting with [small POC] and measuring [business metric].

Worth comparing notes on whether this is already on your 2026 roadmap?
```

For Seth, the message should show regulated-buyer and workflow judgment. It should not read like a model-feature pitch.

## Technical Architecture Template

Use enough technical detail to be credible:

- input channels;
- agent or workflow layer;
- tools/actions the agent can take;
- customer systems it needs to read/write;
- OpenAI model/API surface;
- retrieval or company knowledge if needed;
- permissions and scoped access;
- audit logs;
- human escalation;
- evals/monitoring;
- rollout path from pilot to scaled deployment.

Avoid pretending to be the solutions architect. The goal is to show technical buyer fluency and good enterprise judgment.

## Risk Section Template

Include 3-4 risks:

- technical integration risk;
- data/privacy/security risk;
- user adoption or change-management risk;
- business-model or ROI risk;
- accountability/compliance risk;
- incumbent/vendor alignment risk.

Then make the seller move:

"These risks are exactly why I would not lead with a broad AI transformation pitch. I would start with a contained workflow, clear owner, limited data scope, human review, and measurable business outcome."

## Why OpenAI Section

Do not say only "better models."

Better OpenAI framing:

- breadth across ChatGPT Enterprise, API, agents/workflows, Codex, voice, image/video where relevant;
- developer gravity and familiar end-user brand;
- ability to support both employee productivity and product/workflow buildouts;
- enterprise readiness through governance, admin, permissions, auditability, evals, and deployment controls where current product facts support it;
- credible path from pilot to wider deployment.

Product and compliance claims must be verified against current official OpenAI documentation before recording.

## Seth-Specific Standout Angle

Seth's edge should be:

- account intelligence quality;
- APAC/regional and regulated-buyer judgment;
- ability to map executive, product, engineering, legal, and compliance stakeholders;
- AI-native workflow building rather than generic "I use ChatGPT";
- commercial discipline from Airwallex;
- partnership and multi-stakeholder proof from Boxo;
- source-backed GTM systems from Salescraft/GTM Workspace.

Strong close:

"What I would do differently is treat the assignment like the beginning of a real account plan. I would not just pitch an AI use case; I would build the wedge, the stakeholder map, the credibility path, and the first message I would actually send."
