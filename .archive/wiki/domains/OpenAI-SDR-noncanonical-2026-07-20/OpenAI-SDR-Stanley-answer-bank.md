## Elevator Pitch Pattern

Stanley's pitch structure:

1. Identity and location.
2. Years of B2B tech sales experience.
3. Career sequence.
4. One unifying throughline: never joined steady-state teams, always early/no-playbook.
5. Proof sequence by company.
6. Why OpenAI, with personal AI usage plus APAC build-out motivation.
7. Long-term career framing.

Why it works:

- It creates one coherent story instead of a resume recap.
- It makes the "step back" feel consistent with the candidate's history of early builds.
- It answers why this role before the interviewer has to ask.

Seth adaptation:

- Throughline should be "AI-native APAC enterprise GTM operator who turns messy account signals and regulated-buyer complexity into qualified opportunities."
- Proof sequence should likely be Airwallex, Boxo, Salescraft/GTM Workspace.
- Personal AI proof should be Codex/GTM Workspace/review-first workflows, not Glean.
- Long-term frame should be OpenAI enterprise GTM as the place to learn frontier AI selling from the inside.

## AE-To-SDR Step-Back Pattern

Stanley's answer has four layers:

1. Honest acknowledgement: if optimizing for title or short-term comp, he would stay as AE.
2. Reframe: he is optimizing for who he works with and what he works on.
3. Role quality: conversations are with senior leaders at important APAC organizations about strategic AI adoption.
4. Long game: right room, right product, right learning matter more than fastest title progression.
5. Self-awareness: he has AE experience but has not mastered complex enterprise selling.
6. Merit posture: come in, earn trust, learn properly, progress on merit.

Pushback answers:

- Compensation drop: think in five-year trajectory, not twelve-month earnings.
- No promotion in 18-24 months: ask what is missing and keep earning it.
- Candidates struggle with transition: show prior proof of taking a title step to enter a better learning environment.

Seth adaptation:

## Prospecting Motion Pattern

Stanley's four-stage motion:

1. Research.
2. Prioritise.
3. Sequence.
4. Iterate.

Detailed mechanics:

- Research before outreach.
- Select accounts based on pain likelihood and potential deal value.
- For OpenAI, look for AI-readiness signals: public AI investment, AI hiring, obvious AI use cases, regulated workflow pressure, technical adoption, and executive AI agenda.
- Tier accounts.
- Tier-one accounts get deep, personalized, multi-threaded outreach.
- Lower tiers get lighter touches.
- Use multiple channels: email, LinkedIn, phone, over two to three weeks.
- Every touch should lead with a specific business insight.
- Messaging should be problem-first, not product-first.
- The first touch should make the prospect feel understood.
- Track subject lines, angles, replies, and misses.
- Adjust weekly.

Specific answer snippets to preserve structurally:

- "I would rather spend an extra hour on research than blast a generic list."
- "Not every account deserves the same effort."
- "The goal of the first touch is not to pitch. It is to make the person think this person understands my world."
- "AI helps me prepare faster; it does not replace personalization."
- "A no is still a signal."

Seth adaptation:

- Tie this directly to Salescraft/GTM Workspace: source-backed account research, enrichment, scoring, role-specific messaging, CRM hygiene, and seller handoffs.
- Replace Stanley's Command of the Message/MEDDICC claim only if Seth actually uses those frameworks.

## Targeting And Persona Pattern

Stanley segments OpenAI buying into three motions:

### 1. Org-Wide Productivity

Likely product surface:

- ChatGPT Enterprise or similar governed workspace deployment.

Economic buyers:

- COO.
- Chief Transformation Officer.
- Chief Digital Officer.
- Senior C-suite or one level below.

Champions:

- VP Customer Support.
- Head of Operations.
- Revenue Operations leader.
- Function heads with visible productivity pain.

Core idea:

- The economic buyer may not take cold outreach first. Find the champion who personally feels the pain, then build upward.

### 2. Builder/API Motion

Likely product surface:

- API.
- Developer platform.
- Product-embedded AI.

Personas:

- CTO.
- VP Engineering.
- Head of Product.
- Head of Data Science.
- Startup or scale-up founders.

Conversation:

- Capabilities.
- Latency.
- Model performance.
- Cost per token.
- Product differentiation.

### 3. Governance And Deployment Motion

Likely product surface:

- Frontier.
- Agents.
- Enterprise governance/deployment layer.

Personas:

- CTO.
- CIO.
- Chief AI Officer.
- Senior technical and strategic leaders.

Conversation:

- Deploying and governing AI safely across the organization.
- Moving from experimentation to controlled rollout.

APAC nuance:

- Buying committees can be more hierarchical and top-down than in the US.
- Distinguish real authority from influence.
- Build champion path and executive path deliberately.

Seth adaptation:

- This maps well to the existing JD language: product usage, pilots, developer-led use cases, stakeholder mapping, enterprise readiness, and Account Director partnership.

## Competitor Pattern

Stanley names:

- Anthropic.
- Google.
- Microsoft.
- Meta/open-source models on cost.

Anthropic framing:

- Serious enterprise competitor.
- Enterprise-first posture.
- Strong in coding and document-heavy/compliance-sensitive workflows.
- Do not pretend they are weak.

Google framing:

- Strong distribution via Workspace, Search, Android.
- Serious compute.
- Possible enterprise execution inconsistency due to split attention.

Microsoft framing:

- Historically OpenAI's largest partner.
- Increasingly a competitor as the relationship evolves.

Win themes:

1. Breadth and consumer brand: ChatGPT familiarity lowers adoption friction.
2. Developer ecosystem: default-choice effect, integrations, community knowledge, builder mindshare.
3. Human execution and trust: when models are close, the seller wins by understanding the customer, moving quickly, and earning trust.

Seth adaptation:

- Keep this aligned with the friend briefing: do not lead with "our model is better."
- Use competitor presence as discovery fuel.
- Say "great that you are already using AI" before exploring where OpenAI can land or expand.

## AI Fluency Pattern

Stanley's answer avoids pretending to know transformer internals.

Three-wave model:

1. AI as productivity tool: individual users with ChatGPT, Copilot, Claude for drafting, coding, brainstorming, and basic knowledge work.
2. AI as embedded layer: companies integrate models into products and workflows through APIs. This becomes competitive differentiation, not just productivity.
3. Agentic AI: systems that take actions across tools, research, write, and execute tasks with increasing autonomy.

Sales implication:

- The buyer conversation shifts from "do you want AI?" to "how will your company compete when every competitor has access to similar models?"
- Differentiation comes from intelligent deployment, governance, workflow selection, and business outcomes.

Seth adaptation:

- Add regulated-buyer nuance: human review, permissions, evals, auditability, governance, and measurable deployment outcomes.
- Tie Codex and GTM Workspace to practical use, not abstract excitement.
