## Original Prompt Requirement

The original Loom prompt had a general Part 1 before the strategic customer case. It asked the candidate to introduce themself to the hiring panel and briefly cover:

- why OpenAI;
- why this role;
- what makes the candidate a strong fit;
- why the candidate would thrive in the environment.

That means the intro should stand on its own. It should not depend on DoorDash, IHH, or the case-company choice.

## 60-Second Cut

> Hi, I'm Seth. My path sits at the intersection of enterprise sales, customer workflows, and AI.
>
> I started in law at Cambridge, then moved into tech because I wanted to work closer to how technology changes companies. At Boxo, I learned technical enterprise sales: mapping commercial, product, engineering, legal, compliance, finance, and executive stakeholders around complex platform decisions. At Airwallex, I learned quota discipline: qualification, outbound, follow-up, CRM hygiene, and handoffs.
>
> OpenAI is specific because I use AI as a work system, not just as a writing tool. I have built source-backed account intelligence, company-memory, proposal, deck, and outbound workflows with human review and source trails.
>
> This Strategic BDR role sits where product usage, pilots, developer experiments, and executive curiosity become real enterprise opportunities. I would thrive because I am comfortable with ambiguity and technical buyers, but disciplined about sales basics: pain, stakeholders, trust, proof, and the next step.

## 90-Second Recommended Loom Opener

> Hi, I'm Seth. My path is a little non-linear, but the through-line is that I keep moving closer to the place where enterprise sales, customer workflows, and AI meet.
>
> I studied law at Cambridge, but moved into tech because I wanted to work closer to customers and adoption. At Boxo, I sold a technical platform into banks, fintechs, super-apps, and exchanges, where the work was understanding the buyer map: commercial, product, engineering, legal, compliance, finance, and the executive sponsor. At Airwallex, I got the operating discipline: qualification, outbound, CRM hygiene, follow-up, handoffs, and quota pressure.
>
> OpenAI is specific because I now use AI the way I think enterprises will need to use it: not as a novelty, but as a work system. I have built source-backed account intelligence, company-memory, proposal, deck, and outbound workflows with human review and source trails. That has made me opinionated that the commercial challenge is not "can AI write a better email." It is: which workflow, which owner, which data, which risk, which proof, and what next step makes this real?
>
> That is why this Strategic BDR role makes sense. It sits where product usage, developer experiments, pilots, and executive curiosity become real enterprise opportunities. I would thrive because I am comfortable with ambiguity and technical buyers, but disciplined about sales basics: qualifying pain, mapping stakeholders, earning trust, and handing an Account Director something worth progressing.

## 2-Minute Full Cut

> Hi, I'm Seth. The easiest way to explain my background is that I started in law, moved into enterprise sales, and then started building AI systems around sales and workflow problems I had lived.
>
> I studied law at Cambridge, then moved into tech because I wanted to be closer to customers, risk, buying decisions, and how companies adopt new systems.
>
> At Boxo, I learned technical enterprise sales in a real way. We sold platform infrastructure into banks, fintechs, super-apps, and exchanges, so a deal was never just one champion. I had to map commercial value, product fit, engineering feasibility, legal and compliance concerns, finance approval, and executive confidence. At Airwallex, I learned quota-carrying discipline: ICP focus, qualification, pipeline creation, first-call prep, follow-up, CRM hygiene, and handoffs.
>
> Over the last year, AI became much more practical for me. I started building the GTM systems I wished I had as a seller: source-backed account intelligence, stakeholder maps, proposal and deck workflows, company-memory systems, and outbound support with human review. That changed how I think about enterprise AI. The question is not whether the model is impressive. It is whether you can identify the workflow, owner, data boundary, risk, success metric, and path from experiment to trusted deployment.
>
> That is why OpenAI is specific, and why this role is specific. Strategic BDR at OpenAI is not just volume prospecting. It is a launchpad into enterprise selling where the work is to turn product usage, pilots, developer-led experimentation, and executive curiosity into qualified opportunities an Account Director can progress.
>
> I would thrive because I like high-bar, ambiguous environments, and I am comfortable learning from people sharper than me. I would come in with enough enterprise GTM reps to be useful, enough technical curiosity to learn quickly, and enough humility to keep pressure-testing my judgment.

## Question Cutdowns

| Question | Speakable answer |
|---|---|
| Why OpenAI? | OpenAI is specific for me because it is not just a model company in how I use it. It is becoming a work surface and platform layer for how people research, build, analyze, sell, and operate. The commercial problem underneath that is the one I care about: how do you turn experimentation into a real workflow, with the right owner, risk boundary, proof, and buying process? |
| Why this role? | I like that the role sits close to signal. It is not only activity volume. It is account selection, product usage, discovery, stakeholder mapping, and Account Director partnership. For OpenAI, that feels like the right launchpad because the best sellers will need to understand both technical workflow and enterprise buying motion. |
| What makes you a strong fit? | I have seen both sides of the motion. At Boxo, I learned complex technical enterprise sales with product, engineering, legal, compliance, finance, and executive stakeholders. At Airwallex, I learned disciplined pipeline and quota execution. Then I built AI-native GTM workflows around the exact problems sellers face: account research, stakeholder mapping, proposal prep, and clean handoffs. |
| Why would you thrive here? | I like ambiguous, high-talent environments where the answer is not handed to you. I am not coming in pretending I know the OpenAI playbook. I am coming in with enterprise sales reps, technical curiosity, and a habit of building systems around the work. That combination usually helps me learn quickly and stay useful. |
| Why Strategic BDR instead of AE? | I do not see it as a step back if the role is strategic and the company is OpenAI. I have carried quota and run deals, but here I would rather learn the account motion, product surface, and AD operating model properly than optimize around title. The role is close to the signal that creates great enterprise opportunities. |
| What is your AI proof-of-work? | The clearest proof is that I have built my own GTM and company-memory workflows: source-backed account intelligence, stakeholder maps, proposals, decks, outbound support, and prep systems. The important part is not that AI drafts content. It is that the workflow keeps sources visible, lets a human review, and turns scattered context into usable sales judgment. |
| Did you use AI to prepare? | Yes, heavily, but in a review-first way. I used it to organize source material, pressure-test likely questions, find gaps, rehearse answer versions, and separate sourced facts from assumptions. It did not decide my motivation, invent facts, submit anything, or replace the judgment of what I should actually say. |
| What did AI make better? | It made the prep more exhaustive and more honest. It forced me to see which answers were too vague, where I was over-claiming, and where I needed cleaner evidence. That is also how I think enterprise teams should use AI: to raise judgment density, not remove judgment. |
| What is the bridge into the case? | With that context, I will move into the account strategy. I will keep the case grounded in one workflow, one buyer map, one measurable business outcome, and a 30-day plan to test whether it deserves Account Director time. |

## Case Transition

Use this after the Part 1 opener:

> With that context, I will move into the account strategy. I will keep this practical: why this account, why this workflow, why now, who needs to be involved, how I would qualify it, and what I would hand to an Account Director after the first 30 days.

Use this if the case is DoorDash:

> I will use DoorDash for the case because it is a large, operationally complex customer where AI should be judged by workflow impact, not by whether we can make a clever demo.

Use this if the case is IHH or another open-choice APAC account:

> I will use an APAC regulated-enterprise account because it tests the part of the role I think matters most: moving from AI interest to a governed, stakeholder-backed workflow with a real business case.

## Talk Track Logic

| Prompt bullet | Proof to use | What it should signal |
|---|---|---|
| Why OpenAI | Daily AI use, source-backed workflows, OpenAI as work-system layer | Conviction without brand worship. |
| Why this role | Product signal to enterprise opportunity, AD partnership, discovery and qualification | Understands Strategic BDR is not generic SDR. |
| Strong fit | Boxo technical enterprise sales, Airwallex quota discipline, AI-native GTM systems | Can handle buyer complexity and sales execution. |
| Thrive in environment | Ambiguity, technical curiosity, humility, learning from high-bar team | Self-awareness and coachability. |

## Do Not Say

- Do not name or imply a private warm path.
- Do not imply application submission, referral, or outreach happened unless Seth confirms it.
- Do not say OpenAI is always the best model, cheapest option, or obvious vendor.
- Do not claim insider process details, quota targets, compensation, or specific hiring-manager facts.
- Do not make this sound like "AI writes better emails." The sharper frame is workflow, trust, proof, and enterprise adoption.
- Do not say the Strategic BDR role is only a stepping stone. Say it is the right launchpad because it is close to account signal and AD partnership.
