## One-Screen Takeaway

The reported process is not a generic SDR loop. It appears designed to test whether the candidate is an AE-calibre, consultative seller who is deliberately stepping back for OpenAI, can learn fast, can talk enough AI to be credible, and can turn enterprise AI interest into qualified opportunities without trying to act like a technical seller or a maverick strategist.

The core mold from the briefing:

- Deliberate step back: "I would not take SDR elsewhere; I would do it because this is OpenAI."
- Sales posture: consultative and value-based, leading with buyer priorities, pain, challenges, current state, desired outcomes, and business impact.
- Technical posture: know RAG, hallucination, LLM/agent basics, Copilot, Anthropic, and OpenAI product breadth well enough to speak clearly, but do not pretend to be a deep technical expert.
- Deal posture: walk through one to two deals at high level, emphasizing multi-threading, relationship/rapport, challenge navigation, value framing, close, and lessons learned.
- APAC posture: show real regional nuance, not one-size-fits-all global prospecting.
- Personal posture: humble, serious, warm enough, coachable, not "outside the box" for the sake of it.
- AI posture: they may expect AI-assisted prep and may ask to screen-share the prep doc.

## Round 1: HR Screen

Reported as straightforward and generic.

Covered topics:

- Compensation/base/commission-type conversation.
- Why OpenAI.
- What the candidate is looking for.
- Why the candidate is willing to take a step back into SDR.

Prep implication:

- The step-back answer needs to sound deliberate, not defensive.
- The strongest version is not "I need a job" or "I like AI." It is "OpenAI is a generational enterprise GTM learning environment, and this role sits close to product signal, discovery, account strategy, and Account Director partnership."

## Round 2: Loom Case

Reported format:

- Five to seven minute Loom recording.
- Candidate presents materials with video.
- Slides are not strictly mandatory, but friend strongly recommends them because they make the message clearer.
- PowerPoint, Canva, or similar is fine.
- Friend's example prompt was a company strategy case, with Uber as the example in the source notes and Grab mentioned as a personal-experience customization.
- The case asked how a company should use AI and included one OpenAI use-case pitch.
- The prompt may not be identical, but the structure is expected to be similar.
- The video is reviewed by VP-level sales, not the SDR manager.
- Friend's video was around six minutes.
- The turnaround/request may specify "within three business days."
- Loom allows editing, but awkward cuts/gaps can show.
- Allow a full day for recording and editing.

Prep implication:

- Build a short deck, not a dense written memo.
- Pick a company where the business context, workflows, stakeholders, and OpenAI use case can be made concrete.
- Keep the pitch structured: company context, AI opportunity, workflow/use case, stakeholder value, risk/governance note, why OpenAI, next step.
- Do not copy the friend's deck because it was tailored to his own experience.

## Round 3: SDR Manager

Reported interviewer:

- Source says a US/NA frontline sales-development manager.
- Transcript has noisy name transcription, and user notes refer to "JT." Treat exact identity as unverified.

Reported tests:

- "What do you know about OpenAI?"
- Pitch/explain OpenAI, LLMs, or agents as if explaining to parents.
- Motivation for the role.
- Walk through a closed deal end to end.
- Describe prospecting motion.
- Review the Loom recording and answer follow-up questions.
- Show willingness to learn.
- Show humility and coachability.
- Answer APAC/regional nuance questions.

Product-depth calibration:

- Friend's advice: know your stuff, but do not go too technical in this round.
- The manager is likely testing whether the candidate has credible understanding and can communicate simply.

Prospecting answer structure from the briefing:

1. Start with the customer's priorities and what matters to them.
2. Understand current setup.
3. Identify pain points or challenges.
4. Connect those issues to business impact.
5. Understand the desired future state or ideal setup.
6. Identify the business outcomes they want.
7. Match OpenAI to that outcome.

APAC/regional nuance answer content:

- APAC is not one market.
- India: more cost sensitive, according to the source.
- ANZ and Singapore: more tech-forward and mature, according to the source.
- Channel behavior differs by country.
- Do not assume email and cold calls are enough everywhere.
- Vietnam may require local app/channel awareness such as Zalo.
- Other SEA markets may have different dominant communication habits.
- They may ask: "If you could pick one APAC market to land in first, which would it be and why?"

Prep implication:

- Prepare one APAC market choice with logic. Singapore is the safest default because it aligns with role location, regulated buyers, regional HQ density, public-sector AI ambition, financial services, and English-language enterprise selling.
- Also have one alternative, e.g. Australia/ANZ for maturity, India for scale/developer density but cost sensitivity, or a SEA super-app market for platform workflows.

## Round 4: AE Interview Series

Reported structure:

- Two interviews, each around 30 minutes.
- Intended to be same day/back-to-back within roughly two hours, though scheduling may vary.
- Interviewers were enterprise AEs.
- Source says one interviewer was Australia-based and one was US-based.
- Source says both had around two years at OpenAI.
- One round was technical/product fluency.
- One round was commercial acumen.

### Technical/Product Fluency Round

Reported questions/topics:

- Can hallucination be 100 percent avoided?
- What is RAG?
- RAG came up repeatedly and should be treated as a major topic.
- How would you pitch OpenAI to a prospect already using Copilot?
- How does OpenAI compare with Anthropic?
- How does OpenAI win?
- Why take a step back to SDR?

Time split from the briefing:

- Around 20 minutes on product/technical fluency.
- Around 10 minutes on competitive/prospect pitch questions.
- One to two minutes on genuine curiosity about the step-back decision.

Prep implication:

- RAG and hallucination need crisp, speakable answers.
- The goal is not to out-engineer them. The goal is to show judgment about reliability, retrieval, grounding, evaluation, and enterprise risk.
- Copilot and Anthropic answers should not sound adversarial. Start from what the buyer already uses, then explore workflow gaps.

### Commercial Acumen Round

Reported questions/topics:

- Sales methodology, but likely not by asking for a named framework.
- Walk through a deal end to end.
- How do you prospect?
- What is your target/quota?
- How many run-rate deals do you do monthly or quarterly?
- How many deals do you close per month?
- Average deal size.
- Scenario: if you joined OpenAI tomorrow, which companies would you target first and why?
- What angle or value driver would you land with?
- Anthropic objection: if the prospect already uses Anthropic, why should they consider OpenAI?

Methodology guidance:

- They may not ask "What framework do you use?"
- They will listen for elements of consultative/value-based selling.
- Do not lead with cheaper pricing.
- Do not lead with "our product is superior."
- Do not lead with "OpenAI is wider/better" as the main argument.
- Lead with priorities, pain points, challenges, business impact, desired outcome, and workflow fit.

Anthropic answer from the briefing:

- Acknowledge that the prospect has already started with AI.
- Treat existing Anthropic usage as a positive signal.
- Say the conversation is not rip-and-replace.
- Explore the prospect's priorities and workflows.
- Identify where OpenAI can land within the existing stack.
- Expand from there if value is proven.
- Use OpenAI product breadth carefully, e.g. ChatGPT, API, Codex, agents, Whisper, Sora, company knowledge, governed workspace, or other relevant product surfaces, but tie breadth to workflow need rather than generic superiority.

Copilot answer posture:

- Respect Microsoft distribution and buyer comfort.
- Ask what workflows Copilot solves and what remains unsolved.
- Explore where frontier model capability, API/developer needs, Codex, workflow agents, company knowledge, governance, or specialized use cases may sit beyond bundled productivity.

## Deal Walkthrough

The briefing is very explicit: do not overcomplicate the deal walkthrough.

What they are listening for:

- What you did.
- Whether the approach was value-based or cost/discount-led.
- Whether multi-threading was involved.
- How you built rapport or personal relationships where relevant.
- How you navigated challenges.
- How you closed.
- What you learned.

What to avoid:

- Too much pricing detail.
- Too much internal system/process detail.
- Too much product implementation minutia.
- Any posture that makes the deal sound like cheap-pricing or discount selling.

Deal prep requirement:

- Rehearse one to two deals thoroughly.
- Friend only used one, but Seth correctly noted that interviewers may ask for a second.
- Know numbers cold: average deal size, run-rate pipeline, closed deals per month, quota/target, close rate if relevant.
- Have one primary deal and one backup deal.

Best Seth-fit deal candidates:

- Airwallex: quota, logos, fintech/regulated buyer, high-velocity commercial motion, metrics discipline.
- Boxo: enterprise platform partnership, C-suite/product/engineering/legal/compliance multi-threading, USD 450K ARR, 9 super-app partnerships.
- Salescraft/GTM Workspace: AI-native account intelligence, source-backed outbound, workflow building, USD 1M+ pipeline, USD 200K revenue.

Gap framing:

- Be upfront if a prior role was SME/high-velocity rather than enterprise.
- The briefing says honesty was appreciated.
- Turn the gap into learning posture: "My motion was higher velocity, but it taught me pipeline discipline. The reason I want this role is to learn OpenAI's strategic enterprise motion while contributing my account-intelligence and regulated-buyer background."

## Why SDR / Step-Back Answer

This comes up repeatedly.

The safest answer shape:

1. Acknowledge the title step-down honestly.
2. Explain that OpenAI is a unique enterprise GTM learning environment.
3. Frame the role as a launchpad into Account Director/enterprise selling, not generic SDR volume.
4. Connect it to the JD: product signal, pilots, discovery, stakeholder mapping, deal shaping, AD partnership, AI-enabled GTM workflows.
5. Show humility: "I have sold and built GTM systems, but OpenAI's product surface and enterprise adoption motion are unique enough that I want to learn it properly."

Speakable version:

"I see it as one step back to take two steps forward, but only because it is OpenAI and because this specific SDR role is close to strategic enterprise selling. I have sold and built GTM workflows before, but OpenAI's account motion is different: product usage, pilots, technical champions, governance concerns, and Account Director partnership all have to come together. I would rather learn that motion properly and contribute with my APAC regulated-buyer and AI-native GTM background than force-fit myself into an AE title somewhere less relevant."
